OUR PROJECT / SOIL HEALTHLOOKING BEYOND THE BIN

From e-waste
to soil health.

The E-ternal Reboot explores what happens when electronics affect the environment.

In the project's video, the team describes a plant-growth experiment investigating the effects of e-waste on soil. They also explore biochar, a carbon-rich material that can bind certain contaminants, as a potential way to help affected soil.

Soil contamination needs expert assessment; biochar is not a substitute for safe e-waste disposal or professional remediation.

01 / UNDERSTANDING E-WASTESTART WITH THE BASICS

What exactly
is e-waste?

E-waste is electronic or electrical equipment that is no longer wanted or usable.

It includes everyday items like phones, computers, headphones, chargers, cables and appliances. Batteries also need special handling. Some items can be repaired or reused; others need to go to an authorised recycler.

⌁

Devices

Old phones, tablets, laptops, keyboards and headphones.

↝

Accessories

Unused chargers, cables and other electronic accessories.

✳

Appliances

Electrical appliances and other equipment that no longer works.

02 / WHY IT MATTERSA BETTER ENDING FOR OUR TECH

More than
just rubbish.

Electronics contain a mix of materials. Responsible handling helps keep harmful substances out of the environment and allows useful materials to be recovered.

01✳

Protect people

Breaking, burning or handling electronics unsafely can expose people to harmful substances.

02♡

Care for nature

Improper disposal can pollute soil, water and air. Keep e-waste separate from household rubbish.

03↻

Save resources

Responsible recycling can recover useful materials and reduce the need for new raw materials.

03 / YOUR CHECKLISTBEFORE YOU RECYCLE

Give it a
thoughtful goodbye.

A few careful steps can make all the difference.

First, check whether the device can be repaired, reused or donated. If it is time to recycle, back up and erase your personal data and remove accounts according to the device maker's instructions.

Ask an adult for help finding an authorised collection point. Keep damaged or swollen batteries away from heat and do not open, crush or throw them in a regular bin. Follow local collection guidance for safe handling.
